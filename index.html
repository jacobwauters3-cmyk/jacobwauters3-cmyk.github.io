CASE STUDY 01

Reducing Errors in Marketing Automation

Fictional company · Synthetic data
This case study uses a fictional company and synthetic data. The methodology reflects the type of analytical, operational, and technical marketing work I've done professionally, without using confidential employer information.
Problem

The standard way to keep an automated journey running past its expiration was to copy it and activate the copy. But copying a journey silently reset its contact deduplication rules — a behavior newer team members had no way of knowing about. When they copied and relaunched expired journeys, contacts who had already received that email were sent it again.

Approach

Built a standard operating procedure for journey expiration that made the deduplication reset explicit and non-optional, then added automated QA checks — flags that catch when a contact has already been sent a given email — so the system catches the risk before send, not after.

Current state → redesigned process
Before / after
MetricBeforeAfterChange
Duplicate mis-send incidents / quarter111−91%
Journey relaunches following the SOP~30%100%+233%
Relaunches with an automated dedup flag check0%100%—
New team members onboarded to the processInformal / tribal knowledgeDocumented SOP—
Impact

The root cause wasn't a lack of care — it was an undocumented platform behavior that new team members had no way to anticipate. Documenting it and backing it with an automated flag turned a recurring, hard-to-catch error into a near-zero-incident process.

CASE STUDY 02

Improving Lead Routing for a B2B Marketing Org

Fictional company · Synthetic data
This case study uses a fictional company and synthetic data. The methodology reflects the type of analytical, operational, and technical marketing work I've done professionally, without using confidential employer information.
Problem

The existing lead routing process was not functioning as intended, causing new leads to be assigned to a queue that was not actively monitored or worked. As a result, leads could sit untouched instead of reaching the appropriate sales team, creating delays in follow-up and putting potential opportunities at risk. The issue required coordination across Sales, the Sales Business Office, and CRM product owners to identify the breakdown and establish a more reliable routing process.

Approach

Designed explicit routing logic that checks account match and segment before territory assignment. For leads with no account match, built a zip-code-based fallback so every lead still reached an AE — and passed along the page the lead came from and the products they showed interest in, so AEs had real context instead of a bare name and email.

Impact

Every lead reached an AE — even without an account match — through a zip-code fallback instead of sitting unassigned. Passing along the source page and product interest also meant AEs opened each lead already knowing what the prospect cared about, instead of starting cold.

CASE STUDY 03

Diagnosing a Decline in Email Campaign Performance

Fictional company · Synthetic data
This case study uses a fictional company and synthetic data. The methodology reflects the type of analytical, operational, and technical marketing work I've done professionally, without using confidential employer information.
Problem

Over 12 months, open rate fell from 27% to 19% and conversion rate fell from 1.1% to 0.6%, even as send volume grew. Leadership wanted to know why — and what to do about it.

Approach

Segmented the decline by audience, device, and campaign type instead of treating it as one problem. The drop wasn't even: one segment and one campaign type accounted for most of the loss.

12-month trend
Root cause breakdown
Contributing factorShare of decline
One underperforming segment (re-engagement list)42%
Send frequency increase (+35% sends, same list)27%
Mobile rendering issues on one template19%
Other / seasonal12%
Impact

Nearly 70% of the decline traced to two fixable causes — one list and one template — rather than a broad loss of interest. Recommendation: suppress the re-engagement list from high-frequency sends and fix mobile rendering before making any content or offer changes.

CASE STUDY 04

Channel Strategy for a New Product Launch

Fictional company · Synthetic data
This case study uses a fictional company and synthetic data. The methodology reflects the type of analytical, operational, and technical marketing work I've done professionally, without using confidential employer information.
Problem

A B2B software company was launching a new self-service pricing tool to its existing customer base. The audience wasn't one group — some customers already showed high product engagement, some were active but hadn't touched adjacent features, and some were dormant. A single blast announcement would under-serve all three.

Approach

Built a three-phase channel plan — pre-launch awareness, launch push, post-launch sustain — with channel mix and messaging weighted by segment, and budget allocated toward the channels each segment historically responded to rather than split evenly.

Target audience segments
SegmentDefinitionAddressable sizePrimary channel
High-engagementActive weekly, using 3+ product areas~9,200 accountsIn-app + email
Active, unadoptedActive monthly, hasn't used adjacent tools~14,600 accountsEmail + paid social
DormantNo login in 60+ days~6,100 accountsEmail re-engagement
Three-phase channel plan
Impact

Weighting channel mix by segment instead of running one blast meant high-engagement users got the low-friction in-app nudge that actually moves them, while dormant accounts got the heavier-touch re-engagement email sequence they needed — projected to lift 60-day adoption meaningfully above a single-channel launch.

CASE STUDY 05

Migrating a Brand Site to Enterprise Hosting

Fictional company · Synthetic data
This case study uses a fictional company and synthetic data. The methodology reflects the type of analytical, operational, and technical marketing work I've done professionally, without using confidential employer information.
Problem

One brand's website was hosted outside the company's enterprise environment and built by an external dev agency. When enterprise security updates came due, the agency couldn't apply them — they didn't own or manage that hosting infrastructure. The gap kept widening with every release cycle.

Approach

Built the case for migrating the site onto enterprise hosting around three angles — security compliance, lower hosting cost, and access to shared enterprise assets — then aligned the brand's dev team, the internal security team, and the external dev agency to scope the work, build it out in lower environments first, and cut production over once validated.

The gap → the fix
The case for migration
AngleArgument
SecurityCloses the patching gap — brings the site under enterprise security standards instead of relying on an agency with no access to apply them
CostSeparate external hosting was more expensive than the enterprise environment already being paid for
Shared assetsGains access to enterprise-managed infrastructure, tooling, and components the brand team otherwise had to duplicate
Who owned what
GroupRole in the migration
Brand's dev teamOwned the target enterprise environment; received and stood up the migrated site
Internal security teamDefined the security requirements the migration needed to satisfy; validated compliance before cutover
External dev agencyExecuted the site build and content work, but had no access to apply enterprise-level hosting changes
Impact

Framing the migration around security, cost, and shared assets — not just "we should move this" — got three separate groups with three separate priorities aligned on the same plan. Building it out in lower environments first meant the production cutover happened with the scope already validated, not discovered mid-migration.

CASE STUDY 06

Technical SEO & Site Performance Audit

Fictional company · Synthetic data
This case study uses a fictional company and synthetic data. The methodology reflects the type of analytical, operational, and technical marketing work I've done professionally, without using confidential employer information.
Problem

Organic traffic had been flat for two quarters despite steady content output. Core Web Vitals were failing on mobile, on-page SEO and accessibility hadn't been audited in over a year, and legacy plugins were adding render-blocking scripts no one had flagged.

Approach

Benchmarked Core Web Vitals and ran a full on-page SEO and accessibility audit. Fixed what I could directly in the front-end, partnered with the dev agency on backend changes like schema markup and removing plugins that were hurting performance, and paired it with keyword research, ongoing rank monitoring, and content optimization.
